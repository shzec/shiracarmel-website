02About

Short bio

Shira Z. Carmel is an Israeli-born, Melbourne-based singer, composer and multidisciplinary artist. Working in Hebrew and English (and also Yiddish), she moves between alternative pop, jazz, cabaret, folk and experimental song, often setting poetry to music. Her projects have toured internationally and her work spans film, television, radio, interdisciplinary art and even commercials. Her new single, “Four Boys Maybe Five,” sets a poem by Tal Nitzán for voice, cello, baritone saxophone and piano.

Long bio

For more than two decades, Israeli-born and Melbourne-based artist Shira Z. Carmel has made songs that move between alternative pop, jazz, cabaret, folk and experimental music. A composer, singer and songwriter, she works in Hebrew, English and Yiddish, and has built a varied body of work through vocal ensembles, jazz projects, theatrical song and collaborations across the arts. Her music often interweaves political and humanist themes, drawing on her lifelong work as an activist and volunteer.

Shira was a founding member, songwriter and co-arranger of the vocal trio The Hazelnuts, which toured internationally. Other projects include the Shira Z. Carmel Quartet, her brass band Shira Z. Carmel and her Brasserie, the Yiddish experimental duo The Technicalities, and “Infinity and One — Lullabies for Mothers,” setting music to poetry about motherhood. Her work has also appeared in film, television, radio and commercials.

Her latest single, “Four Boys Maybe Five,” sets a poem by Tal Nitzán in a minimal chamber arrangement for voice, cello, baritone saxophone and piano. The poem’s two mirrored scenes ask a parent to imagine both sides of a violent encounter. Recorded and produced in Melbourne, the song arrives from a distance that offers space to listen closely without looking away.

03The song

New single

Four Boys
Maybe Five

Poem by Tal Nitzán · Music by Shira Z. Carmel

Voice, cello, baritone saxophone and piano shape a dark, cinematic chamber song that holds the poem's moral question in close focus: what changes when the child in danger is yours, and what changes when your child is among the others?

Tal Nitzán’s poem appeared in The Guardian’s “Poem of the week” in September 2024. Read the poem in The Guardian ↗︎
